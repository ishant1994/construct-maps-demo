Antibodies against HSV-1 and complement

Does complement engagement contribute to protection by antibodies against HSV-1 in neonatal mice, and does it do so across antibodies?

Background

This case study was sourced from the paper “Interaction with complement factor C1q can contribute to and improve monoclonal antibody-mediated protection against lethal HSV-1 challenge” by Jimenez et al. 1 Antibodies bound to a virus can start the classical pathway of the complement system by binding C1q, and HSV resists complement, since its glycoprotein gC binds C3b and blocks the cascade 1. Antibodies against gD and gB, the glycoproteins that attach the virus to cells and fuse it with them, protect animals, and effector functions such as Fc receptor engagement contribute in some cases, but the mechanisms are not fully defined 1. Jimenez et al. test the role of complement with antibodies whose Fc either cannot bind C1q (K322A) or promotes antigen-dependent IgG hexamers that favor C1q binding and complement activation (E430G) 1.

The plugin was then asked this question and told to use only literature published before 2026-01-01. Jimenez et al. appeared after that date, and neither library cites it. Below is what GPT and Claude proposed, then what Jimenez et al. found and how the proposals compare.

The prompt given to GPT and Claude

Design a library of anti-HSV-1 antibody constructs against gD and gB to test whether complement engagement contributes to protection in neonatal mice and whether it does so uniformly across antibodies. Only use literature published before 2026-01-01.

GPT's library has ten constructs built on four antibodies, HSV8 and CH42 against gD and D48 and BMPC-23 against gB, each with its own wild-type Fc. Every antibody gets K322A, which removes a C1q contact and, in an HIV antibody, abolished C1q binding and complement-dependent killing while also reducing FcγR binding affinity twofold 2. HSV8 and D48 also get E430G, which promotes IgG hexamers on antigen and raises complement activation 3. The antigen-binding regions are identical within each antibody, so any difference in protection comes from the Fc. GPT plans to repeat each comparison in mice that lack C1q, to tell a complement effect from another Fc effect.

Claude's library has ten constructs built on three antibodies, E317 against gD and D48 and BMPC-23 against gB, each as wild type, K322A 4 and E430G 3. It adds an Fc-silenced E317 (LALA-PG), which removes both C1q and Fc receptor binding and shows how much of E317's protection depends on Fc functions. Reading the same pair of changes across three antibodies shows whether complement matters equally for each. The design draws on the finding that neutralization and Fc effector functions both contribute to protection by gD antibodies against HSV-1 in neonatal mice 5, and on the fact that HSV evades both complement and antibody effector functions through gC, which binds C3, and gE, which binds IgG Fc 6.

What Jimenez et al. found

Jimenez et al., in a preprint posted in October 2026, built seven antibodies (E317, HSV8 and CH42 against gD, and Hu2c, HDIT102, D48 and BMPC-23 against gB) as wild type, K322A and E430G and tested them in 2-day-old mice challenged with HSV-1 1. The Fc changes did not alter antigen binding, and K322A abolished C1q binding for every antibody and C3b deposition for all but BMPC-23, whose K322A form kept reduced but detectable C3b deposition 1.

In lab strain 17, protection differed by antibody. K322A removed all of E317's protection, and E430G added nothing for it. E430G improved survival for HSV8, and K322A's drop for HSV8 was not significant. CH42 was unaffected by either change. For the gB antibodies, K322A made no difference and E430G significantly improved survival for all four, while wild-type BMPC-23 was not protective 1. With the clinical isolate NS, fewer pups treated with the K322A form of Hu2c survived 1. In mice lacking C1q and challenged with NS, the effects of K322A and E430G disappeared for HSV8, Hu2c and HDIT102, so they ran through C1q 1.
