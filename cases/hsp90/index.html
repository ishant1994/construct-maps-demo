ATP binding and hydrolysis in Hsp90 client maturation

In yeast, Hsp90 that cannot hydrolyze ATP still supports growth, but Hsp90 that cannot bind nucleotide does not. Which step does a given client protein need?

The prompt

Design a library of HSP90 variants to distinguish the roles of ATP binding and ATP hydrolysis in client maturation.

The library has eight full-length versions of yeast Hsp82, each a homodimer, numbered as in Hsp82 (E33 is E47 in human Hsp90α). It starts from the published finding that Hsp82-E33A, which binds ATP but has no detectable ATPase activity, supports yeast growth 1, while the nucleotide-binding-defective D79N does not 2. Wild type, D79N and E33A are the core comparison. Earlier work found that the client kinase v-Src inhibited the growth of E33A cells less than normal, which indicates impaired v-Src activation 1, so viability alone does not show how well a client matures. E33A/E372K and E372K add the published suppressor, which rescues E33A's growth defects without restoring detectable ATP hydrolysis 2, together with its single mutant as a control, so a rescue can be told from a general effect. In vitro, ATP-γ-S stabilizes the closed clamp of E33A/E372K much more than that of E33A 3. K98A and E33A/K98A ask whether returning to the ADP state matters more when hydrolysis is impaired. The published E33A/K98A double mutant barely grows 2, so the open question is its effect on client maturation. R380A keeps about half of wild-type ATPase activity but cannot form the closed clamp in a docking assay 3, so it asks whether ATP turnover is enough without clamp closure.
