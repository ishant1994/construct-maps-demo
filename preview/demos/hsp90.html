Hsp82: ATP binding versus hydrolysis in client maturation

Distinguish nucleotide occupancy, hydrolytic chemistry, conformational coupling and nucleotide-state resetting in client maturation. Use a defined yeast Hsp82 homodimer background and client-specific function alongside loading, release and stability; do not use viability or ATPase as substitutes for maturation.

WT Hsp82Native homodimer reference

709 aa2 polypeptides

Reference chosen because the binding, hydrolysis, suppressor and ADP-affinity mutations were compared in this isoform. No purification tag, coiled coil, reporter cysteine, client or cochaperone is implicit.

D79NAdenine-contact substitution

709 aa2 polypeptides

Hypothesis

Does nucleotide occupancy contribute to maturation beyond client binding by an apo scaffold?

Caveats: D79N affects nucleotide binding broadly, not ATP alone, and can also alter conformation. It is not an ADP-binding-preserved ATP-selective perturbation.

Proposed edit

Substitute 1 residue in Nucleotide-binding NTD: D79N. Retains Charged linker, Client/coupling MD, C-terminal assembly region and MEEVD docking motif.

As described Disrupt the adenine-contact site while retaining the complete dimeric scaffold.

Evidence

  • D79N nucleotide-binding-defective variants failed to support yeast viability, whereas E33A variants retained condition-dependent function.

    Yeast Hsp82 and other Hsp90 orthologs expressed as the sole Hsp90 in plasmid-shuffle assays. This is a cellular complementation endpoint, not a direct client-maturation measurement. · Results, Figure 1; Table 3

    Nucleotide exchange is sufficient for Hsp90 functions in vivo

  • Human Hsp90alpha E47A failed GR ligand-binding recovery after Hsp70 inhibition, but Bag-1-assisted Hsp70 release allowed E47A to maintain GR function.

    Reconstituted human Hsp90alpha/Hsp70/HOP/p23, yeast Ydj1 and GRLBD(F602S); this distinguishes release from maintenance in that system. · Results, Figures 4D and 6B

    Glucocorticoid receptor function regulated by coordinated action of the Hsp90 and Hsp70 chaperone cycles.

709 aa2 polypeptides

Hypothesis

Can ATP occupancy support client maturation when hydrolysis is below detection?

Caveats: E33A alters conformational equilibria and analog responses as well as chemistry. A defect is not proof that hydrolysis energy is the direct missing requirement; retained viability is not proof of normal client maturation.
