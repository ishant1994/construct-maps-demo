Residue 94 and the β7–β8 loop in S. aureus sortase A

Why do substitutions at P94 raise the activity of Staphylococcus aureus sortase A, and is the β7-β8 loop involved?

Background

Sortase A from Staphylococcus aureus joins proteins that carry the motif LPXTG, which makes it a standard tool for protein ligation, but the wild-type enzyme is slow. Directed evolution produced a variant with five substitutions (P94R, D160N, D165A, K190E, K196T) that is 115-fold more efficient, and how those substitutions work is not known 1. Structures show a loop next to the active site, β7–β8, that is closed in the inactive enzyme and open in the active one, and a contact between Y187 in the loop and P94 in a nearby helix is thought to hold it closed. The paper asks whether breaking that contact explains why substitutions at P94 raise activity 1.

We asked the plugin this question using only literature published before 2026-01-01, so it could not have seen the paper. Below is what GPT and Claude proposed, then what the paper found and how the proposals compare.

The prompt we gave GPT and Claude

Design a library of Staphylococcus aureus sortase A (ΔN59) variants to investigate why P94 substitutions raise activity and whether the β7-β8 loop is involved. Only use literature published before 2026-01-01.

GPT's library has eight constructs on the ΔN59 catalytic domain with a His tag. It tests P94R, one of five substitutions in an evolved enzyme that is about 120-fold more efficient 2, against P94S and P94A, to see whether arginine is needed at position 94. It tests Y187L and Y187F, to ask whether the ring or the hydroxyl of Y187 matters. Y187 is the loop residue a 2025 thesis reported in contact with P94 3, and Y187L was chosen because a screen on the evolved enzyme found it, with E189R or E189A, to raise activity further 4. The P94R/Y187L double mutant asks whether the two effects depend on each other, and C184A, the catalytic cysteine, is a control for product formed without enzyme activity.

Claude's library has nine constructs on the same domain without a tag. Its core is the evolved five-substitution enzyme (P94R, D160N, D165A, K190E, K196T) 5 next to the same enzyme with the native proline restored at 94, so the difference between them is what P94R adds when the other four are present. The four are expected to matter because, in S. pneumoniae sortase, alanine at the end of the β7–β8 loop raised activity more than two-fold 2. The library also has P94A, P94S, P94D and P94R in wild type, to separate loss of the proline ring from the charge of the replacement, plus Y187A and P94S/Y187A, to ask whether the effect of residue 94 depends on Y187.

What the paper found

The paper, a preprint posted in August 2026, asked why substitutions at P94 raise sortase activity 1. Kinetics showed that P94D alone matches the evolved enzyme with the LPATG substrate (kcat/Km of 750 ± 60 and 745 ± 85 M⁻¹ s⁻¹), and that changing Y187 moves activity both ways: Y187R was about two-fold more active than wild type, Y187A about half as active, and Y187D nearly inactive 1.

NMR on catalytically inactive C184A versions added information about binding and the loop. Dissociation constants for the LPATG peptide were about 10 mM for C184A, 0.4 mM for P94D/C184A and 0.5 mM for the C184A evolved enzyme. In P94D/C184A the signals of loop residues D186, Y187 and N188 could not be reliably identified and those of V193 and W194 shifted, which the authors read as P94D changing the dynamics of the β7–β8 loop 1.
