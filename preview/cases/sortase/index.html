Residue 94 and the β7–β8 loop in S. aureus sortase A

Yeast-display evolution of Staphylococcus aureus sortase A 1 produced a variant with five substitutions (P94R, D160N, D165A, K190E, K196T) that is about 120-fold more efficient 2. Two of them, K190E and K196T, are in the β7–β8 loop next to the active site, a loop that moves when substrate binds 2. P94 is in a nearby helix, and a 2025 thesis, not peer reviewed, reported a hydrophobic contact between P94 and Y187 in the loop 3. That leaves open whether P94R acts through this contact or independently of the loop and the other four substitutions.

The prompt we gave GPT and Claude

Design a library of Staphylococcus aureus sortase A (ΔN59) variants to investigate why P94 substitutions raise activity and whether the β7-β8 loop is involved. Only use literature published before 2026-01-01.

The prompt already names P94 and the β7–β8 loop, so the models did not have to find them.

The two libraries approach this from opposite directions. GPT keeps wild-type sortase and varies residue 187 along with residue 94, which shows whether the effect of P94R depends on what sits in the loop. Claude varies the rest of the enzyme and keeps residue 94 to two states, proline or arginine, which shows whether P94R still adds anything once the evolved enzyme's other substitutions are present. Together they ask whether P94 acts through the Y187 contact or depends on the rest of the protein.

Do the effects of P94R and Y187L depend on each other?

GPT pairs double with both single mutants, so what matters is how far the double mutant departs from additivity in kcat/Km (on a log scale), not either single effect. GPT chose Y187L because a screen of the evolved enzyme found it, together with E189R or E189A, to raise activity further 4(Fig. 2, Table 1). Y187L removes the aromatic ring and keeps a hydrophobic side chain, and y187f, which keeps the ring and loses only the hydroxyl, shows which part of the tyrosine matters.

The design record notes two limits. In that screen, Y187L helped only together with E189 changes in the evolved enzyme, so Y187L alone in wild type has not been tested. And a non-additive result can also come from a change in the rate-limiting step instead of a shared contact.

How much does residue 94 add once the other four substitutions are present?

If m4-p94 is nearly as active as m5, the other four substitutions account for most of the gain and P94R adds little. If it is close to wild type, P94R carries most of the gain. An intermediate result would mean both contribute.

The four substitutions are expected to matter because of the loop. In S. pneumoniae sortase, replacing the last residue of the β7–β8 loop, or one near its start, with alanine increased conversion more than two-fold, and the authors suggest that an interaction between the loop and β6 slows the enzyme 2(Fig. 6). For S. aureus sortase they report only that K196 sits near D160 in several states of the NMR structure 2. m4-p94 carries K196T and D160N, so the library applies this argument to S. aureus, where it has not been tested by mutagenesis. The design record also notes that this four-substitution enzyme has not been reported with the native proline at 94 and may fold or express poorly.

Kinetics linked the gain to residue 94, and NMR added evidence about the loop

The paper, posted in August 2026, measured wild type, P94D and the evolved enzyme with the LPATG substrate 5. P94D alone matched the evolved enzyme (kcat/Km of 750 ± 60 and 745 ± 85 M⁻¹ s⁻¹), so on this substrate one substitution at residue 94 accounts for the whole gain 5. Changing Y187 moved activity both ways. At 20 minutes, Y187R was about two-fold more active than wild type, Y187A about half as active, and Y187D nearly inactive 5. These results link residues 94 and 187 to catalytic efficiency, but efficiency alone can't say whether binding or catalysis changed, or what the loop is doing.

NMR on catalytically inactive C184A versions of the enzymes addressed both questions. The dissociation constants for the LPATG peptide were about 10 mM for C184A, 0.4 mM for P94D/C184A and 0.5 mM for the C184A pentamutant, so P94D and the evolved enzyme bind substrate about equally well, 20 to 25 times more tightly than the parent 5. In P94D/C184A, the signals from loop residues D186, Y187 and N188 could not be reliably identified because of line broadening or large shifts, and V193 and W194 shifted. These changes persisted with substrate bound 5. The authors interpret this as P94D changing the dynamics of the β7–β8 loop, and propose that breaking the P94–Y187 contact helps the loop open 5.

Neither library could have produced the binding measurements. Apart from GPT's C184A control, both libraries contain active enzymes, and the binding measurements needed an inactive C184A background. GPT includes C184A only as a no-nucleophile control and warns against using it as a substitute for measuring substrate binding, which is how the paper used it 5. Both libraries do propose a way to look at the loop. Claude proposes following the W194 indole N–H by 15N-HSQC across the reference, P94S, Y187A and p94s-y187a, and W194 was one of the residues that shifted in the paper. GPT proposes local NMR or HDX measurements for y187l and y187f.

The paper measured three of Claude's constructs: p94d, y187a and m5. It did not test m4-p94, and it approached the same question from the other direction by placing one substitution in wild type. It did not test GPT's double mutant either, so the paper addresses whether the effect of P94 depends on residue 187 only through single substitutions and the NMR changes 5.
