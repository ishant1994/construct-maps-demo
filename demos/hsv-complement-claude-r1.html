Anti-HSV-1 gD and gB IgG1 library for testing complement engagement in neonatal mice

Test whether C1q-dependent complement engagement contributes to antibody-mediated protection of neonatal mice from HSV-1, and whether any contribution is shared by gD- and gB-directed antibodies that differ in neutralizing ability.

The unmodified human IgG1 form of the gD-directed, receptor-blocking antibody; every Fc variant of this antibody is measured against it.

Hypothesis

K322A removes C1q binding while the Fab and the FcgR-binding lower hinge are unchanged. If complement contributes, this variant protects less than wild-type at a sub-saturating dose by more than any loss of neutralization explains.

Caveats: K322A was defined against human, rabbit and guinea-pig complement, so loss of mouse C1q binding on gD- or gB-bound IgG is assumed and must be measured. Neonatal mouse C3 is low, so a null result cannot separate no role from too little complement to matter. If the antibody binds C1q poorly at baseline, K322A has nothing to remove (the 10-1074 paper cites b12, whose wild-type form already behaved as complement-null). K322A also cannot separate C1q acting alone (steric interference with attachment, as reported for influenza stem antibodies) from downstream cascade activation.

Proposed edit

Heavy chain: Substitute 1 residue in CH2 and C1q-contact loop (CH2): K327A. Retains Heavy variable domain (antigen recognition), CH1 (heavy-light pairing), Hinge, Lower hinge (FcgR contact), CH3 (Fc-Fc and dimer interface) and CH3 loop at the FcRn / gE-gI site.

As described Removes the lysine at the centre of the human IgG1 C1q footprint while leaving the Fab and the FcgR binding region untouched.

Hypothesis

E430G favours Fc-Fc contacts between neighbouring antigen-bound IgG, so hexamers form more readily on antigen and C1q avidity rises. If complement is limiting for this antibody, protection at a sub-saturating dose exceeds wild-type with the Fab, neutralization and FcgR binding unchanged.

Caveats: E430 lies in the CH3 loop beside H435, part of the site bound by FcRn and by the HSV-1 gE-gI Fc receptor, and the effect of E430G on gE-gI or mouse FcRn binding has not been reported for HSV antibodies, so a change in protection could not yet be assigned to complement alone. Hexamer formation needs enough antigen at the right spacing; gD is likely sparser than gB on the virion, and gC (C3b binding) and gE-gI can offset the gain.
