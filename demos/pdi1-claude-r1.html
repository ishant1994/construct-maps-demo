Pdi1 residue library: which residues drive ribosome binding

Identify which residues of the wasp proline-rich antimicrobial peptide Pdi1 drive its binding to the bacterial ribosome, separating binding determinants from determinants of uptake and potency.

Pdi1 (reference)

20 aa

Native 20-residue Apidaecin 1+ of Pimpla disparis (UniProt C0HKX8), as free acid. The natural processed 1- form lacks residues 1-3.

Hypothesis

Comparator for every variant. Its ribosome-arresting activity is measured, but the contribution of each residue to binding is not.

Caveats: No pre-2026 study measures which Pdi1 residues bind the ribosome. Every residue-level role used here is transferred from Api, Api137, Fva1 or drosocin, and the Pdi1 sequence differs from Api between positions 3 and 12.

Evidence

  • Synthetic Pdi1 inhibited E. coli BL21 at 0.125 µM in 33% TSB and 1 µM in RPMI/serum, against 0.5 and 4 µM for Api, and K. pneumoniae AR-0112 at 1 and 16 µM; it was among the most potent peptides tested.

    Broth microdilution of the synthetic peptide. · Table 1

    Activity, structure, and diversity of Type II proline-rich antimicrobial peptides from insects

  • Pdi1 produced a stop-codon toeprint like Api and Api137 and a GFP stop-codon readthrough halo, the two hallmarks of release-factor trapping.

    PURExpress toeprinting on a yrbA template and the pRXG(UGA) reporter in E. coli BL21. · Fig. 3 and Fig. 4

    Activity, structure, and diversity of Type II proline-rich antimicrobial peptides from insects

  • The peptide was isolated from the hemolymph of P. disparis as a 20-residue form and a 17-residue form lacking the first three residues, with different activity spectra.

    Protein sequencing and MALDI mass spectrometry, as curated by UniProt. · UniProt C0HKX8 curation
